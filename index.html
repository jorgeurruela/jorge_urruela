Graduate research · Kitchin Research Group, CMU · Feb 2026–present

Differentiable flowsheets: rare-earth separation and refinery optimization

I'm testing a JAX-based, fully differentiable sequential-modular flowsheet library (unreleased) on real process problems. Because the whole flowsheet is differentiable, optimizers get exact end-to-end gradients instead of finite differences.

  • Rare-earth separation (Gd from Y/Ho; 10-stage extractor and 15-stage scrubber with PC88A; 4 decision variables). I reformulated the problem to remove non-convex and flat regions and switched from gradient-free Powell to gradient-based SLSQP. That lifted the two product purities from 86% / 86% to 98.7% / 90.5%, and SLSQP converged in 2–5 min where trust-region took 20 min without converging.
  • Runtime: a better initial guess for the recycle solver plus restricting the search to the non-flat operating range cut simulation time by more than 50%. That made it practical to generate a 20,000-point training set overnight.
  • Surrogate: a two-headed neural network (shared 32→32 layers, one 16-node head per purity) reached validation R² ≥ 0.9994 on both outputs, and a larger version reached 0.9999. It runs hundreds of thousands of evaluations per second. It beat two separate networks of the same size, and oversampling the optimal region recovered usable accuracy from only 3,100 points.
  • Benchmark against Aspen HYSYS on a naphtha hydrotreater. Most stream and temperature results matched within 0.01–5%, and the flowsheet converged in 0.13 s vs 0.3 s. On a CAPEX + OPEX sizing problem, gradient-based SLSQP reached the same optimum as Powell 27× faster (1.2 s vs 32.6 s).

Process Systems Modeling · Spring 2026 · individual, self-proposed project

Water-treatment network design as an MINLP

I designed a network that routes five contaminated water streams (four contaminants) through up to five treatment units at minimum cost. I wrote my own concentration-based formulation in GAMSPy, using a superstructure of splitters and mixers, binary unit selection with big-M constraints, and economies-of-scale capital cost. It selects two units and reaches US$348,337. That matches the published optimum for the benchmark, which the reference reached with different formulations.

Advanced Process Systems Engineering · Spring 2026 · individual project

Crude-oil scheduling as a continuous-time MINLP

I formulated and solved, in GAMS, a refinery crude-oil pooling and scheduling problem: vessels unload to storage tanks, which feed charging tanks, which feed the distillation units. The model sets the timing of every transfer under blending, capacity and changeover constraints to minimize cost. The optimum came to US$221k, and changeovers drove 45% of it. Sensitivity runs on vessel arrival, changeover cost, demand and tank capacity showed how tightly constrained the network is.

Capstone · Reactor Design, Plant Design & Process Economics · Aug–Nov 2023 · team of 5

Invert sugar plant: kinetics to full plant design

We designed a plant that turns refined white sugar into invert sugar syrup (≈50% glucose, 47% fructose, 3% sucrose) by enzymatic hydrolysis with invertase, sized for US$50M in annual sales.

  • Kinetics: fitted a Michaelis–Menten model with substrate inhibition to lab data (Km ≈ 0.064 M, Vmax ≈ 2.5×10⁻³ M/min) and used it to size the reactor and run sensitivity analyses on feed concentration, flow and temperature.
  • Key design decision: free invertase leaves with the syrup and would have cost about US$1.08M a year. We moved to enzyme immobilized in calcium-alginate beads in a fluidized-bed reactor at 60 °C, so the enzyme stays in the reactor and is replaced quarterly. That brought enzyme cost down to about US$10k a year, a 99% reduction.
  • Plant: dissolution → decolorization (activated carbon / diatomaceous earth) → cooling → enzymatic inversion → evaporation to 75–77 °Brix → storage. We produced the P&ID, PFD, equipment specification sheets, plant and site layouts, a 3D model, and the environmental-permit requirements (Guatemala, Acuerdo Gubernativo 137-2016).

Thermodynamics II · 2nd semester 2022 · team project, I led the program design

Regenerative Rankine cycle simulator (Excel + VBA)

An interactive tool that solves a regenerative steam cycle with one open feedwater heater from user inputs. The user enters the boiler, extraction and condenser pressures, the turbine inlet temperature and the turbine and pump efficiencies, and the tool returns every state point.

  • Calculates h, s, T and P at all 7 states, plus the extraction fraction, cycle efficiency compared with Carnot, turbine work, condenser heat duty and pump work.
  • Four ways to specify the heater: feedwater temperature, approach to saturation, extraction fraction, or the optimum extraction.
  • Input validation based on thermodynamics: it rejects a turbine inlet that isn't superheated, impossible temperature crosses and entropy decreases, and explains why.
  • Teammates tested it and wrote the user manual.
  • Alongside it, I built a reusable library of Excel worksheet functions in VBA for the course. It covers SRK and Peng–Robinson α-functions, generalized-virial residual properties, Cp and ΔG integrals, Antoine, Riedel and Margules.

Pilot-scale ethanol fermentation

Our group's goal was high-purity ethanol. My partner and I ran the fermentation stage. We used about 166 kg of sugar solution and fed it in three batches a week apart into the same vessel, so the yeast was never hit with all of the substrate at once.

  • We tracked conversion daily through °Brix and analyzed the growth behavior against Monod kinetics.
  • We saw inhibition in the third batch once ethanol reached about 3 wt%. That limited the final titer and shows why staged feeding matters.

Methods Engineering II · April 2021 · team of 3 · report in English

Shelving-shop simulation: removing a bottleneck

A shelving manufacturer (case study) had low order fill rates and frequent stockouts. We used time-study data to build a discrete-event model of its 33-station line (back panel, frame and shelf subassemblies feeding final assembly) and tested improvements before recommending any.

  • Fitted processing-time distributions per station and shelf type, validated the base model, and found the bottleneck stations.
  • Compared three alternatives over 6-week runs: adding a station, relieving bottleneck operators of material handling, and staggered shifts.
  • Recommended the two no-capital options combined, and a supplier change based on a statistical comparison of wood rejection rates.

Operations Research · Nov 2021 · team of 3

Allocating scarce pickup-truck supply with linear programming

During the 2021–22 chip shortage, a vehicle importer was assigned only 453 units against 912 units of annual demand across four pickup lines. We built an LP model (Excel Solver, Simplex) to schedule monthly orders and inventory to maximize profit. The pure optimum (~US$2.36M) dropped the entry-level line entirely. We argued that this would hurt long-term market position, and the final recommendation kept that line in the mix.

Statistical Data Analysis & Simulation · June 2022 · team of 5

Road traffic analysis: where and when to invest

Used UK road-traffic datasets to decide whether new roads, upgrades or maintenance windows were needed. EDA in Python, dashboards in Power BI, and a Monte Carlo arrival simulation in R (exponential inter-arrivals, 200 runs). Result: no urgent capacity need, maintenance best between 4 and 6 AM, and growth in car and taxi traffic that policy should shift toward buses and bicycles.

Numerical Methods · Sept 2020 · with Pedro Estévez

Desalination mass balance: Gauss–Seidel vs. Jacobi

We solved the mass balance of a reverse-osmosis desalination process as a linear system, rearranged it to be diagonally dominant, and compared the two iterative methods. Gauss–Seidel converged in 27 iterations, far fewer than Jacobi. The short paper covers the trade-off: Gauss–Seidel is faster but more prone to divergence.
